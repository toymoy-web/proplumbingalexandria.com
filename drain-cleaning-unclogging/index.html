Local Cause

Why Drains Clog in Alexandria Homes

Kitchen and bathroom drains clog from ordinary buildup, grease, hair, and soap scum, the same in any city. Alexandria's specific pattern shows up in main sewer lines: pre-1960s homes in Rosemont and Del Ray often still run on clay or cast-iron laterals. Mature street trees throughout those neighborhoods send roots into any small crack in that older pipe, and the result shows up as a recurring backup rather than a one-time clog.

Signs to Watch For

Signs of a Clogged Drain or Sewer Line

Slow Drainage

Water taking longer than usual to clear a sink, tub, or shower is often the first sign of a building clog.

Gurgling Sounds

Air bubbling back up through a drain when another fixture runs usually means a partial blockage further down the line.

Multiple Fixtures Backing Up

If more than one drain backs up at the same time, the problem is likely in the main line, not an individual fixture.

Recurring Clogs

A drain that clears but clogs again within weeks, especially in an older home, points toward root intrusion in the sewer lateral.

How We Diagnose It

How the Clog Is Diagnosed

For a single slow fixture, a technician usually cables the line directly and confirms flow is restored. For a recurring or main-line clog, a sewer camera inspection is run first to see exactly where the blockage or root intrusion sits, since guessing at a spot repair on an older clay lateral without seeing the actual damage tends to miss the real problem.

Repair Options

Cabling vs. Hydro Jetting

Cabling

  • Lower cost
  • Clears most standard clogs
  • Faster for single-fixture backups
  • Doesn't remove buildup on pipe walls

Hydro Jetting

  • Clears grease and root intrusion fully
  • Scours the full pipe diameter
  • Longer-lasting result
  • Recommended for recurring clogs

Before You Call

What to Check Before You Call

Note Which Fixtures Are Affected

Tell us whether it's one drain or several, since that narrows down whether the clog is local or in the main line.

Stop Running Water

If a backup is active, avoid running more water into the system until a technician arrives.

Don't Use Chemical Drain Cleaners on a Full Backup

Chemical cleaners rarely clear a full blockage and can make the eventual repair more hazardous to work on.
