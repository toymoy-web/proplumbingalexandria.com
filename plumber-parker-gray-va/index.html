Before Help Arrives

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak

Most Parker-Gray homes are row houses that share walls, so water from one house can reach a neighbor quickly. Here is what to do while help is on the way.

Shut Off the Main Valve

Stop the water at its source first. In a row house the shutoff is often near where the line enters at the front or rear.

Kill Power to Wet Areas

Switch off power to the wet area only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.

Clear the Immediate Area

Move people, pets, and valuables away, and warn a neighbor if water is moving toward a shared wall.

Call Our 24/7 Line

Emergency dispatch answers around the clock, so there is no bad hour to call, even with a neighbor's wall at risk.

Neighborhood Overview

Plumbing in Parker-Gray, Alexandria

Parker-Gray is the Uptown-Parker-Gray Historic District, which fills most of the northwestern quadrant of the Old Town street grid laid out in 1797. The district runs roughly from Cameron Street north to First Street and North Columbus Street, with Buchanan Street and North West Street forming its western line. It was listed on the Virginia Landmarks Register in 2008 and the National Register of Historic Places in 2010.

The name comes from the Parker Gray School, which opened in 1920 and honored two principals of segregated schools in the late 1800s. Queen Street was once the city's primary African American business district, and the Alexandria Black History Museum and the Charles Houston Recreation Center still anchor the neighborhood. Hooffs Run Park and Greenway is nearby.

The National Register listing counts 984 contributing buildings, mostly small row houses and townhouses with corner businesses mixed in. Frame houses with Italianate and Queen Anne details sit in the core of the district. Whole blocks in the southwest and the western half are brick Colonial Revival row houses, and more than 200 public housing units were built as Colonial Revival row houses between the early 1940s and 1959.

What Plumbing Calls Look Like Here

Attached Row Houses

Row houses share party walls, so a leak at one address can show up as a stain next door. A plumber's written diagnosis of where the failure started helps sort out who pays.

Historic District Review

Parker-Gray is a Board of Architectural Review district. Visible exterior changes, such as a new vent or a tankless exhaust, may need approval before work starts, so ask the city first.

Compact Layouts

Narrow row houses leave little room around water heaters and shutoffs. Access is worth checking before you plan a replacement.

Mixed Eras on One Block

Late 1800s frame houses stand near brick row houses from later building campaigns, and years of renovation mean each house can hold a different mix of pipe. A camera or careful inspection shows what you actually have.

Before You Call

What Parker-Gray Homeowners Should Know

Ask About Exterior Changes

Before scheduling non-emergency work that touches an exterior wall or roofline, check whether the Board of Architectural Review needs to see it. Emergency repairs go ahead first.

Photograph the Damage

Pictures of the leak and any water that crossed a shared wall help both households and their insurers. Take them before cleanup if it is safe.

Know Your Shutoff

Find the main shutoff while nothing is wrong. Everyone in the house should know where it is.
