Water damage restoration covers the cleanup after water has already gotten into a home, not the plumbing repair that caused it. That distinction matters in Alexandria, where a burst supply line, a failed sump pump, or a Potomac-adjacent storm surge can each soak a basement in a single afternoon. This crew runs a 24/7 emergency line specifically for restoration calls, separate from general plumbing repair, because standing water starts causing structural and mold damage within 24 to 48 hours.

Local Cause

Why Alexandria Basements Flood

Old Town and the low-lying blocks near the Potomac waterfront sit on a naturally high water table, so basements there see groundwater pressure that a healthy sump pump usually manages and a failed one does not. Further inland, in Rosemont and Seminary Valley, older homes built before 1960 often still run galvanized supply lines that corrode from the inside out. Those lines can split without warning, usually behind a wall or under a slab, and the leak can run for hours before anyone notices.

Signs to Watch For

Signs You Have a Water Damage Problem

Standing Water

Visible pooling in a basement, crawlspace, or around a water heater or washing machine.

Musty Odor

A persistent damp smell often means water has already reached drywall, subfloor, or insulation.

Discolored Drywall or Ceiling

Yellow-brown staining or bubbling paint signals moisture behind the surface.

Warped Flooring

Hardwood cupping or laminate lifting at the seams is a common sign of slow, ongoing water intrusion.

How We Diagnose It

How the Crew Diagnoses the Damage

The first step on any call is finding the water source, not just the water itself, using moisture meters and thermal imaging to trace how far it has spread behind walls and under flooring. Once the extent is mapped, extraction equipment removes standing water, and industrial air movers and dehumidifiers run a structural drying cycle that typically takes three to five days depending on how much material absorbed water. Work follows the IICRC S500 water damage restoration standard, the industry reference for how drying and moisture verification should be documented and completed.

Before You Call

What to Do Before the Crew Arrives

Shut Off the Water Source

If the source is a burst pipe or failed appliance, shut off the nearest valve or the main shutoff to stop more water from entering.

Photograph the Damage

Take photos of standing water and affected walls or flooring before any cleanup starts, for insurance documentation.

Move What You Can

Lift or relocate furniture, rugs, and boxes off a wet floor if it is safe to do so without wading through standing water near outlets.

Don't Run the HVAC System

Running central air or heat can spread airborne moisture and mold spores to unaffected rooms before drying begins.
