Sewer line repair covers the pipe running from the house to the city connection, typically the source of recurring backups that cabling alone can't permanently fix. In Alexandria's older neighborhoods, this line is often original clay or cast-iron pipe from before 1960, and the repair path depends heavily on what a camera inspection actually finds underground.

Local Cause

Why Sewer Lines Fail in Alexandria's Older Neighborhoods

Rosemont, Del Ray, and parts of Old Town were built when clay and cast-iron were the standard sewer lateral material, and both degrade over 60-plus years through joint separation and corrosion. Mature street trees throughout these neighborhoods send roots toward any moisture escaping through a small crack or loose joint, and what starts as a minor gap gradually widens into a full blockage or collapse.

Signs to Watch For

Signs of a Failing Sewer Line

Recurring Backups

A sewer that clears with cabling but backs up again within weeks usually means an underlying pipe defect, not just debris.

Multiple Drains Affected at Once

Every fixture on the lowest level backing up simultaneously points to the main line, not an individual drain.

Soggy or Sunken Yard Patches

A wet or unusually green patch of yard along the line's likely path can indicate an underground leak.

Sewage Odor Indoors or Outdoors

A persistent sewage smell, especially near a cleanout or floor drain, often means a break somewhere in the line.

How We Diagnose It

How the Line Is Diagnosed

A sewer camera is run through the line from an accessible cleanout to locate the exact position and type of damage, whether that's root intrusion at a joint, a localized crack, or a fully collapsed section. That footage decides which repair method applies since a small, localized crack in an otherwise sound line is a very different job from a line that has collapsed across multiple sections.

Repair Options

Spot Repair vs. Trenchless Replacement

Spot Repair

  • Lower cost for localized damage
  • Faster turnaround
  • Minimal excavation
  • Best for a single crack or joint failure

Trenchless Replacement

  • Full line replacement without full excavation
  • Solves widespread root intrusion
  • Longer-lasting than repeated spot fixes
  • Recommended for older, fully degraded lines

Before You Call

What to Know Before the Camera Inspection

Know Your Cleanout Location

If you know where the sewer cleanout is on your property, mention it when you call. It speeds up the camera inspection.

Note Recurring Patterns

Tell us if backups happen after heavy rain specifically, since that can point toward a cracked line letting groundwater in.

Avoid Repeated Cabling Alone

If a line has needed cabling more than twice in a year, a camera inspection is worth doing before paying for another temporary clear.
