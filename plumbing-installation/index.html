New plumbing installation covers everything from a single new fixture during a kitchen remodel to full rough-in plumbing for an addition or new construction project. Most installations in the City of Alexandria require a permit through the Department of Code Administration, which this team handles as part of the job rather than leaving it to the homeowner to sort out separately. Coordinating that permit alongside the actual construction schedule is often the part homeowners appreciate most, since it avoids a separate scramble once the general contractor is already on-site.

Local Cause

Common Installation Projects in Alexandria

Kitchen and bathroom remodels in Old Town and Rosemont's older rowhouses often require relocating supply and drain lines to fit a new layout, since original plumbing was rarely placed where a modern renovation wants it. Newer construction around Potomac Yard and Carlyle sees more straightforward rough-in work following current code from the start, though even new-build projects benefit from confirming fixture placement early rather than after drywall goes up.

How We Diagnose It

How the Installation Process Works

The technician reviews the planned layout, pulls any required permit, and schedules rough-in work to align with the rest of the renovation timeline, since plumbing typically needs to be inspected before walls close up. Final fixture connections happen once flooring and finishes are in place. Coordinating inspection timing with the general contractor's schedule is usually the single biggest factor in avoiding delays on a larger project. All rough-in work follows the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, which incorporates the International Plumbing Code (IPC).
