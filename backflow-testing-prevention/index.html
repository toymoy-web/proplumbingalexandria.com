Backflow Testing & Prevention in Alexandria, VA

Annual backflow preventer testing and repair to keep irrigation and commercial water systems compliant with Virginia cross-connection control requirements.

A backflow preventer stops contaminated water from flowing back into the clean municipal supply, and Virginia's cross-connection control rules require annual testing for properties with an irrigation system, fire suppression line, or certain commercial connections. This is a compliance-driven service more than a symptom-driven one, tested on a schedule rather than waited on until something visibly fails.

What Backflow Prevention Actually Protects Against

Most property owners haven't thought much about backflow prevention until a water utility notice arrives requiring annual test documentation. The device itself prevents a pressure drop in the main line, from firefighting nearby or a water main break, from siphoning irrigation or industrial water back into the drinking supply.

Local Cause

Who Needs Backflow Testing in Alexandria

Properties with an irrigation system, a fire sprinkler line, or certain commercial connections typically have a backflow preventer already installed as a code requirement, and Virginia's water utilities require annual testing with documentation submitted to confirm the device is functioning. A device that fails testing needs repair or replacement before the property remains in compliance.

Signs to Watch For

When You Need Testing or Service

Annual Test Notice

Most municipal water providers send an annual reminder when testing is due, based on the device's installation date.

New Irrigation System

A newly installed irrigation system needs a backflow preventer installed and tested before connection to municipal water.

Visible Leaking at the Device

Water discharging from the device's relief valve when not actively testing can indicate a failed component.

Real Estate Transaction

Some property transactions require confirming the backflow device is current on testing.

How We Diagnose It

How Testing Works

A certified technician connects test equipment to the device's check valves and relief valve, verifying each holds the required pressure differential. Results are documented on the standard test form, which is typically submitted directly to the water utility to keep the property's compliance record current.

Repair Options

Testing vs. Repair

Annual Test

  • Required yearly in most cases
  • Confirms device is functioning
  • Documentation submitted to utility
  • Lower cost when device passes

Repair or Replacement

  • Needed when a device fails testing
  • Restores compliance
  • New device installation available
  • Retested after repair
