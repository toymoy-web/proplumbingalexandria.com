Commercial leak detection follows the same electronic tracing methods used residentially, but with an added focus on locating the source with as little disruption to a business's operating hours as possible. This is a common request from Old Town retail spaces and Carlyle office buildings dealing with an unexplained utility bill spike or a suspected leak in a shared wall or ceiling.

Local Cause

Where Commercial Leaks Commonly Show Up

Multi-tenant buildings often have shared plumbing chases where a leak in one unit's wall isn't immediately visible in the affected space itself, showing up instead as water damage in a neighboring unit or common area. Older commercial buildings converted from residential use in Old Town sometimes still carry original supply lines not originally designed for commercial-scale water demand. A leak in one of these older conversions can go unnoticed longer than in a newer building, since shared walls and finished ceilings hide the early signs that a homeowner would otherwise catch quickly. Property managers overseeing several such buildings often find this pattern repeats across their portfolio.

Signs to Watch For

Signs of a Commercial Leak

Unexplained Utility Bill Increase

A water bill spike with no change in usage patterns is the most common trigger for a commercial leak call.

Water Stains in Shared Areas

Staining on a ceiling or wall in a common area can indicate a leak in an adjacent unit or shared chase.

Tenant Complaints

Multiple tenant reports of dampness or odor in a multi-unit building often point to a shared-wall leak.

Reduced Water Pressure

A drop in pressure across a commercial space can indicate a leak drawing off supply somewhere in the building.

How We Diagnose It

How Commercial Detection Works

The technician uses the same acoustic and thermal detection equipment as residential calls, scaled to the building's layout, often scheduled after hours for retail and office spaces to avoid disrupting business during the day. Findings are documented for property management, ownership, or insurance purposes, and any resulting repair follows Virginia's plumbing code requirements (IPC).
