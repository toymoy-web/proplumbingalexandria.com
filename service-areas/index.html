Alexandria Pro Plumbing dispatches technicians across the entire City of Alexandria, VA, from the 18th-century rowhouses of Old Town to the newest construction in Potomac Yard. Every neighborhood below has its own real housing history and plumbing considerations, covered in more detail on each area's own page. Alexandria's housing stock spans roughly 275 years of construction, and that range shows up directly in what each neighborhood calls about most: pre-1960s rowhouses in Old Town, Rosemont, and Del Ray deal with galvanized pipe age and sewer root intrusion, while newer developments like Carlyle, Eisenhower East, and Potomac Yard see mostly warranty-period fixture issues and shared-building leak detection in their all-modern plumbing. Knowing which category your neighborhood falls into is often the fastest way to understand what kind of plumbing problem you are likely dealing with before a technician even arrives. Call (703) 912-0567 for same-day or 24/7 emergency dispatch anywhere on this list. Metro access varies meaningfully by neighborhood too: Old Town, Carlyle, and Eisenhower East all sit on the Blue and Yellow lines, Potomac Yard has its own station that opened in 2023, while Seminary Hill, Beverley Hills, Cameron Station, and Landmark rely on bus service rather than direct Metro access. This mix of transit-oriented and car-dependent neighborhoods is part of what makes Alexandria's plumbing landscape genuinely varied rather than uniform across the city, and it is exactly why each area page below covers its own real housing details rather than a generic citywide description repeated eleven times. ZIP coverage follows the same neighborhood boundaries closely, though a few ZIPs, including 22301, 22304, and 22314, span more than one neighborhood on this list, so the specific area page for your address is the more precise reference than the ZIP code alone. Housing density also varies considerably across this list: Old Town and Rosemont pack hundreds of rowhouses into a compact, walkable footprint, while Cameron Station and West End include a mix of single-family homes, townhomes, and garden apartments spread across more land area. That density difference affects everything from how quickly a technician can reach a given address to how a shared plumbing issue in a multi-unit building gets diagnosed compared to a standalone house on its own separate supply and drain lines, with no neighbors' plumbing involved at all. Whatever your neighborhood's housing era, every repair here is completed to the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code's plumbing standards (IPC), and a technician provides an upfront cost before any work begins. Plumbing Service Areas in Alexandria, VA

Prefer to talk it through first? Call (703) 912-0567 and describe what's going on.

Old Town

Alexandria's original 18th-century seaport core, centered on King Street between the Potomac waterfront and the King Street-Old Town Metro station. Colonial-era street grid laid out in 1749.

→

Del Ray

Founded in 1894 as the Town of Potomac, annexed by Alexandria in 1930. A streetcar-era commuter suburb built around Mount Vernon Avenue, now a walkable commercial corridor.

→

Rosemont

An 84-acre National Register historic district developed 1908-1930 as an early streetcar suburb, adjacent to Alexandria Union Station.

→

Seminary Hill

Named for the Virginia Theological Seminary (founded 1823) at the top of Alexandria's highest hill. Mostly rural until postwar development.

→

Carlyle

Alexandria's most contemporary neighborhood, built on former rail-yard land starting in the 1990s. Mixed-use, high-rise residential and office district.

→

West End

The broad western section of Alexandria east of I-395, including Alexandria West and Seminary Hill areas, anchored by Inova Alexandria Hospital.

→

Beverley Hills

A fully planned 1930s residential community of roughly 600 homes in northwest Alexandria, developed with deliberate attention to its rolling, tree-lined terrain.

→

Cameron Station

A 164-acre former U.S. Army quartermaster depot (established 1941), redeveloped starting in 1999 into a planned residential community of roughly 2,000 homes built in five phases.

→

Landmark

The area around the former Landmark Mall site, east of I-395 in Alexandria's West End, now undergoing major redevelopment including a planned new Inova Alexandria Hospital campus.

→

Eisenhower East

A newer mixed-use district anchored by the Eisenhower Avenue Metro station, part of Alexandria's ongoing transit-oriented development south of Old Town.

→

Potomac Yard

A 7.5-million-square-foot mixed-use development on former rail-yard land, with its own Metro station opened May 19, 2023, and Virginia Tech's Innovation Campus anchoring the north end.

→
