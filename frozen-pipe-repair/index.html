A frozen pipe is a race against time even before it cracks, since ice expanding inside a pipe can split it from the inside, and the actual leak often doesn't show until the ice thaws and water starts flowing through the break. This is a genuinely seasonal call in Alexandria's colder months, especially in older homes with pipe runs in exterior walls or unheated crawlspaces.

Local Cause

Why Alexandria Sees Frozen Pipes Despite Being Mid-Atlantic

Alexandria's winters don't reach the sustained deep freezes of colder regions, but a hard cold snap combined with an uninsulated pipe run in an exterior wall or an unheated crawlspace, common in Rosemont and Del Ray's older construction, is enough to freeze a line overnight. Homes built without today's insulation standards are more exposed to this than newer construction with better-protected plumbing runs.

Signs to Watch For

Signs of a Frozen Pipe

No Water From a Fixture

A specific faucet or fixture producing no water while others work normally often means the line feeding it is frozen.

Frost on an Exposed Pipe

Visible frost on a pipe in a basement, crawlspace, or exterior wall access panel is a direct sign of freezing.

Odd Smell From a Drain

A frozen drain or vent line can push sewer gas smell back into the home.

Bulging Pipe Section

A visibly bulging section of pipe means ice has already expanded it, and a crack may be imminent or already present.

Pipe Frozen and No Water?

Safe professional thawing avoids the cracks a space heater or open flame can cause.

How We Diagnose It

How Frozen Pipes Are Handled

The technician locates the frozen section and thaws it using controlled heat methods designed to avoid the rapid, uneven heating that causes a weakened pipe to crack during thawing itself. If a crack is already present, whether discovered before or during thawing, the repair happens immediately rather than leaving the line to fail later.

Repair Options

Thawing vs. Repair After a Crack

Safe Thawing

  • Restores flow without repair
  • Controlled heat prevents new cracks
  • Fastest resolution
  • Best when caught before cracking

Crack Repair

  • Needed once ice has split the pipe
  • Same-visit repair in most cases
  • Insulation added to prevent recurrence
  • Common after a hard freeze

Before You Call

What to Do If You Suspect a Frozen Pipe

Don't Use an Open Flame

Never use a blowtorch or open flame to thaw a pipe. Uneven heating is a common cause of cracks during DIY thawing attempts.

Shut Off the Main If Water Appears

If water starts flowing unexpectedly as ice melts, shut off the main valve immediately in case a crack is present.

Keep the Area Warm

Opening a cabinet door under a sink near an exterior wall can help prevent the line from freezing further while waiting.

FAQs

Common Questions

Using a hair dryer on low, gentle heat is generally safe for an accessible pipe. Open flames and high-heat methods risk cracking the pipe further and are not recommended.

If water starts leaking as the area warms, or you hear dripping inside a wall, the pipe has likely already cracked and needs repair, not just thawing.

Adding pipe insulation to exposed runs in exterior walls, basements, and crawlspaces is the most effective prevention, along with keeping cabinet doors open near exterior-wall plumbing during hard freezes.
