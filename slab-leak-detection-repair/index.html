A slab leak is a supply line leak under a home's concrete foundation, and it's one of the more unsettling calls a homeowner makes, since the first sign is often a warm spot on the floor or an unexplained water bill with no visible source at all. Electronic detection finds the exact location before any concrete gets broken up.

What a Slab Leak Actually Feels Like

Most people don't know to call it a slab leak, they notice a warm patch on the floor, a hissing sound from underneath, or a water bill that's climbed for no obvious reason. Those symptoms together are the clearest sign of a leaking hot or cold supply line running beneath the concrete foundation.

Local Cause

Why Slab Leaks Happen in Alexandria

Copper supply lines under a slab can develop pinhole leaks from soil chemistry reacting with the pipe over decades, and homes near the Potomac with a history of minor foundation settling see additional stress on these under-slab lines from ground movement. Older homes with original under-slab plumbing from before modern protective sleeving was standard are more prone to this than newer construction.

Signs to Watch For

Signs of a Slab Leak

Warm Spot on the Floor

A noticeably warm patch on flooring, especially over a slab, usually means a hot water line is leaking underneath.

Sound of Running Water

A hissing or running water sound with no fixture in use is a common sign of a slab leak.

Unexplained High Water Bill

A slab leak can run continuously, showing up first as a spike in the water bill before any visible sign appears.

Cracks in Flooring or Walls

Water undermining a slab over time can eventually cause visible cracking in flooring or nearby walls.

How We Diagnose It

How Slab Leak Detection Works

The technician uses acoustic listening equipment and thermal imaging to trace the leak's location through the slab, narrowing the search to a small, specific area rather than an exploratory search across the entire floor. Once located, access is limited to that confirmed spot for the repair.

Repair Options

Repair Approaches

Spot Repair

  • Access limited to leak location
  • Faster, lower cost
  • Best for a single, isolated leak
  • Most common outcome

Reroute

  • Bypasses the slab section entirely
  • Avoids repeated slab access if more leaks develop
  • Recommended for older, leak-prone lines
  • Slightly higher cost, more durable

Before You Call

What to Note Before You Call

Check for Warm Spots

Walk the home barefoot to feel for any unusually warm patches on the floor.

Listen for Running Water

In a quiet room with fixtures off, listen for any hissing or running water sound from the floor.
