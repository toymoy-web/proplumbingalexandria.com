A dripping faucet or a sink that won't shut off completely is one of the most common calls this team gets from Alexandria's older rowhouses, where original shutoff valves and fittings from the 1940s and 1950s have been corroding quietly for decades. Most faucet and sink repairs are diagnosed and fixed in a single visit, whether the issue is a worn cartridge, a corroded valve, or a fitting that finally gave out.

Local Cause

Why Alexandria Faucets and Valves Fail Early

Homes in Old Town and Del Ray built before the 1960s often still have their original brass shutoff valves, and decades of mineral buildup from municipal water leaves them stiff, corroded, or seized entirely by the time a fixture needs service. Newer builds around Carlyle and Eisenhower East see a different pattern: modern cartridge faucets that wear out their internal seals within 8 to 12 years of daily use, which shows up first as a slow drip rather than a full leak.

Signs to Watch For

Signs a Faucet or Sink Needs Repair

Persistent Drip

A faucet that keeps dripping after being fully closed usually means a worn cartridge or washer.

Valve Won't Shut Off

A shutoff valve that won't fully close, or won't turn at all, is a corrosion problem common in pre-1960s homes.

Water Around the Base

Moisture pooling at the base of a faucet or under a sink often points to a failed seal or cracked supply line.

Low Pressure at One Fixture

Reduced flow from a single faucet, rather than the whole house, usually traces to a clogged aerator or a partially closed valve.

How We Diagnose It

How the Repair Is Diagnosed

A technician first isolates whether the problem is the faucet itself, the shutoff valve, or the supply line fitting behind it, since all three can produce a similar drip or leak at the fixture. On older Alexandria homes, that often means checking the condition of the original valve stem before touching the faucet. A stiff or corroded valve can turn a straightforward cartridge swap into a bigger job if it fails while the water is isolated.

Repair Options

Repair vs. Full Replacement

Repair

  • Lower upfront cost
  • Fixes cartridge, washer, or seal issues
  • Keeps existing fixture and finish
  • Best for fixtures under 10 years old

Full Replacement

  • Resolves corroded valve or body issues
  • Fresh finish and updated fixture
  • Fewer future service calls
  • Recommended for fixtures 15+ years old

Before You Call

What to Check Before You Call

Locate the Shutoff Valve

Find the valve under the sink before calling, since a stuck valve is useful information for the technician to know in advance.

Note When It Started

A drip that started suddenly versus one that's worsened over months points to different likely causes.

Check for Water Damage

Look under the cabinet for warped wood or staining, since prolonged leaks under a sink can go unnoticed for weeks.
