A garbage disposal that hums but won't spin, leaks from underneath, or has gone completely silent is usually fixed or replaced in a single visit. This is a common call in kitchens across Alexandria regardless of home age, since disposals wear out on a predictable schedule no matter what decade the rest of the plumbing dates to.

Local Cause

Why Disposals Jam or Fail

Most jams come from fibrous food waste, like celery or corn husks, wrapping around the flywheel, while leaks typically develop at the mounting assembly's seal over several years of use. In older Alexandria kitchens with original under-sink plumbing from a prior renovation, the connection to the drain line sometimes needs adjustment when installing a new disposal, since sizes and mounting styles vary between manufacturers. A mismatch between an old drain trap and a new disposal's outlet is a common, easily overlooked detail during a swap.

Signs to Watch For

Signs Your Disposal Needs Attention

Humming But Not Spinning

A humming sound with no grinding usually means the flywheel is jammed and needs to be freed.

Leaking From Underneath

Water pooling in the cabinet below the disposal usually points to a failed seal at the mounting assembly.

No Response at All

A disposal with no sound or response may have tripped its internal reset button or failed electrically.

Unusual Grinding Noise

Metal-on-metal grinding sounds can mean an object is caught inside or the motor bearing is wearing out.

How We Diagnose It

How the Repair Is Diagnosed

The technician first checks the reset button and tests the flywheel for a jam before assuming the motor itself has failed, since these two simple fixes resolve many calls without needing a replacement at all. If the unit is leaking or the motor has genuinely burned out, replacement with a correctly sized unit for the sink and usage is usually the more durable fix, installed to current plumbing code standards (IPC).

Repair Options

Repair vs. Replacement

Repair

  • Lower upfront cost
  • Fixes jams and reset issues
  • Keeps existing unit
  • Best for units under 8 years old

Replacement

  • Resolves leaks and motor failure
  • Option to upgrade horsepower
  • Quieter, more efficient units available
  • Recommended for units 10+ years old

What to Check Before You Call

Check Under the Sink
