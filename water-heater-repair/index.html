Local Cause

Why Water Heaters Fail Early in This Market

Municipal water in the Alexandria area carries enough mineral content to build sediment inside a tank over years. That sediment layer insulates the burner or element from the water it's supposed to heat, forcing the unit to work harder and fail sooner than its rated lifespan. Tanks in unconditioned basements or garages near the Potomac's humidity also see faster external corrosion at the tank seams than units kept in a conditioned space.

Signs to Watch For

Signs Your Water Heater Needs Repair

No Hot Water

A complete loss of hot water usually points to a failed heating element, igniter, or thermostat.

Rusty or Discolored Water

Rust-colored hot water often signals internal tank corrosion, which can mean the tank is nearing the end of its life.

Popping or Rumbling Noises

Sediment buildup rumbling as the unit heats is a common early warning sign, especially in tanks over 6 years old.

Water Around the Base

Moisture pooling at the tank's base can mean a failed drain valve, or in some cases, a cracked tank that needs replacement.

How We Diagnose It

How the Repair Is Diagnosed

The technician checks the thermostat, heating element or gas burner assembly, and pressure relief valve to isolate the actual point of failure, since a unit with no hot water and a unit with lukewarm, inconsistent hot water usually point to different components. On a tank showing visible corrosion or a crack, the conversation shifts honestly to replacement, since repairing a failing tank shell isn't a lasting fix.

Repair Options

Repair vs. Replacement

Repair

  • Lower upfront cost
  • Fixes element, thermostat, or valve issues
  • Extends life of a sound tank
  • Best for units under 8 years old

Full Replacement

  • Resolves tank corrosion permanently
  • Option to upgrade to tankless
  • Improved energy efficiency
  • Recommended for units 10+ years old

Before You Call

What to Check Before You Call

Check the Pilot or Breaker

For gas units, confirm the pilot light is lit. For electric units, check that the breaker hasn't tripped, before assuming the unit itself has failed.

Note the Unit's Age

Check the manufacture date on the tank's label if you can access it, since this changes the repair-versus-replace conversation.

Look for Active Leaking

If water is actively leaking from the tank itself, shut off the water supply to the unit and the power or gas before calling.
