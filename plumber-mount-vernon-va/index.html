Neighborhood Overview

Plumbing in Mount Vernon, Alexandria

Mount Vernon is a Fairfax County community in the far southeast of the Alexandria mailing area, named for George Washington's estate, whose farmland it once was. Route 1 runs along the north, Little Hunting Creek forms the eastern edge, and Dogue Creek crosses the western part.

Neighborhoods such as Wessynton, Yacht Haven, Grist Mill, Mount Vernon Terrace, Woodlawn Park, and Woodlawn Manor fill ZIP 22309. Homes range from the 1950s and 1960s through current builds, with townhome and condo communities from the mid-1980s mixed in.

Woodlawn, a Federal-style house designed by Dr. William Thornton and built between 1800 and 1805, stands on Richmond Highway and became the National Trust for Historic Preservation's first historic site in 1951. The three-mile Mount Vernon Memorial Highway Trail links Route 1 to the estate.

Local Plumbing Challenges

1950s and 1960s Houses

Mid-century homes can still have original cast-iron drains and older copper or galvanized supply lines that have aged out together.

Creekside Moisture

Lots near Little Hunting Creek and Dogue Creek can see damp basements and crawlspaces, and sump pumps carry more of the load.

Water Heaters at End of Life

Tank water heaters typically last 8 to 12 years, so any unit installed before the mid-2010s is worth checking.

Long Service Lines

Larger lots mean longer sewer and water lines from the street, and a longer run means more places for a root or a joint to fail.

Mount Vernon ZIP 22309 Coverage

Most Mount Vernon addresses use ZIP 22309, while ZIP 22121 is a PO Box ZIP tied to Mount Vernon Memorial Highway. Every residential block of 22309 is on this team's routes, from Woodlawn along Richmond Highway to the creekside streets near Little Hunting Creek. Smaller houses north of Route 1 often call about aging supply lines and drains, while larger lots to the south lean toward sewer lines and sump pumps. What Mount Vernon Homeowners Should Know

Know Where the Main Valve Is

On a larger lot the water main may enter far from the house. Find the shutoff before an emergency and label it so anyone in the household can use it.

Watch the Water Bill

A sudden jump on a long service line can mean a leak between the meter and the house. Leak detection can find it without digging up the yard.
