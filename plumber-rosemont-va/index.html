Why Rosemont Calls Us Most

Root Intrusion in Original Sewer Laterals

An 84-acre National Register historic district developed 1908-1930 as an early streetcar suburb, adjacent to Alexandria Union Station. That 450-plus-home streetcar suburb predates modern PVC sewer pipe entirely, so the mature tree canopy that gives Rosemont its character is also the leading cause of sewer backups here: roots find their way into original clay and cast-iron laterals through aging joints.

ZIP Coverage

Rosemont ZIP 22301 & 22314 Coverage

Rosemont's historic district sits mostly within ZIP 22301, with its southeastern edge near King Street extending into ZIP 22314. Both ZIPs see the same root intrusion pattern in original clay and cast-iron sewer laterals, tied to the neighborhood's 1908-1930 construction window and mature tree canopy. Sewer camera inspection and hydro jetting requests come from across both ZIPs about equally, since the underlying pipe age and tree coverage don't change much block to block. Technicians bring camera equipment to every Rosemont sewer call by default, given how often root intrusion turns out to be the underlying cause.
